## 23/06/2026 [ Day 2: Rules, and Hello Linux ]

Today had two completely unrelated halves, which is apparently just how this course is going to work.

**Half 1 -  Security frameworks, or "the rulebooks."** Companies don't just wing their security -  they follow published frameworks so auditors, insurers, and customers can all agree they're not being reckless.
- **NIST Cybersecurity Framework** -  a US government-backed set of guidelines built around five functions: Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover. Extremely widely used, even outside the US.
- **ISO/IEC 27001** -  an international standard for running an "Information Security Management System" (ISMS). A company can actually get *certified* against this one, which is basically a framed certificate that says "we have policies and we (mostly) follow them."

**Half 2 -  Meet Linux, your new best friend.** Nearly every serious security tool either runs natively on Linux or runs best there, so getting comfortable in a terminal isn't optional in this field.

- Linux is open-source and free, and comes in flavours called **distributions** -  Ubuntu (friendly, beginner-ish), Kali (pre-loaded with security tools, what we'll mostly be attacking *from*), Arch (for people who enjoy suffering, no offence to anyone personally invested in that).
- The terminal is king here. Clicking around a GUI is for tourists; real work happens by typing commands.
- Basic navigation, the "hello world" of Linux:
  - `pwd` -  print working directory, i.e. "where on earth am I right now."
  - `ls` -  list what's in the current folder.
  - `cd foldername` -  change directory, i.e. walk into a folder.
  - `mkdir foldername` -  make a new folder.

Nothing glamorous yet -  this is the equivalent of learning to hold a controller before the game actually starts.
